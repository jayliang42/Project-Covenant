## 希伯来诗歌的特点 | Features of Hebrew poetry

旧约有大约三分之一是诗歌。希伯来诗歌不靠押韵，最常见的特点是**平行**：两行或几行放在一起，后一行用不同的话重复、对照或推进前一行。<br>
About a third of the Old Testament is poetry. Hebrew poetry does not depend on rhyme. Its most common feature is **parallelism**: two or more lines placed side by side, where the later line restates, contrasts with, or builds on the earlier one.

- **重复**：“诸天述说神的荣耀；穹苍传扬他的手段”（诗 19:1）。<br>
  **Restating**: “The heavens declare the glory of God. The expanse shows his handiwork” (Psalm 19:1).
- **对照**：“敬畏耶和华是知识的开端；愚妄人藐视智慧和训诲”（箴 1:7）。<br>
  **Contrasting**: “The fear of Yahweh is the beginning of knowledge; but the foolish despise wisdom and instruction” (Proverbs 1:7).

读诗歌的时候，不要把每个词都拆开分析。诗人用很多图像，例如“神啊，我的心切慕你，如鹿切慕溪水”（诗 42:1），要读出图像传达的感受。<br>
When you read poetry, do not pull every word apart. Poets use many images, such as “As the deer pants for the water brooks, so my soul pants after you, God” (Psalm 42:1). Read for the feeling the image conveys.

## 读诗篇 | Reading the Psalms

诗篇共 150 篇，分成五卷（1–41，42–72，73–89，90–106，107–150 篇）。它们原本是以色列敬拜时唱的诗歌和祷告。常见的几类有：赞美诗、感恩诗、哀歌、君王诗、智慧诗。<br>
The Psalms number 150 and are arranged in five books (Psalms 1–41, 42–72, 73–89, 90–106, and 107–150). They were originally songs and prayers used in Israel's worship. Common types include praise, thanksgiving, lament, royal psalms, and wisdom psalms.

**哀歌**是诗篇里数量最多的一类。它们通常有几个部分：向神诉苦（“耶和华啊，你忘记我要到几时呢？”），向神求助，最后表达信靠和赞美（“但我倚靠你的慈爱……我要向耶和华歌唱”）。诗篇 13 篇只有六节，就有这几部分。<br>
**Laments** are the largest group in the Psalms. They usually have several parts: a complaint to God (“How long, Yahweh? Will you forget me forever?”), a request for help, and finally trust and praise (“But I trust in your loving kindness ... I will sing to Yahweh”). Psalm 13 has all of these in just six verses.

诗篇教人把真实的情绪带到神面前，包括愤怒、恐惧和疑问。很多基督徒用诗篇来祷告。<br>
The Psalms teach people to bring real emotions to God, including anger, fear, and questions. Many Christians use the Psalms as their own prayers.

## 读智慧书 | Reading the wisdom books

**箴言**收集了很多简短的智慧话语，讲说话、工作、金钱、交友、家庭。箴言讲的是生活通常的规律。它们并不保证每个人都会这样。《箴言》26:4-5 把两句看似矛盾的话放在一起：一句说不要照愚昧人的话回答他，一句说要照他的话回答他。意思是要看情况判断。<br>
**Proverbs** collects many short sayings about speech, work, money, friendship, and family. Proverbs describe how life usually works. They are not guarantees for every person. Proverbs 26:4–5 puts two apparently opposite sayings side by side: one says not to answer a fool according to his folly, and the next says to answer him according to his folly. The point is to judge which fits the situation.

**约伯记**写一个义人无故受苦，三个朋友用“好人有好报、坏人有恶报”的公式指责他。全书说明，这个公式不能解释所有的苦难。最后约伯说：“我从前风闻有你，现在亲眼看见你”（伯 42:5）。<br>
**Job** tells of a righteous man who suffers for no reason he can see, while three friends accuse him using the formula “the good are rewarded and the wicked are punished.” The book shows that this formula cannot explain all suffering. At the end Job says, “I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you” (Job 42:5).

**传道书**反复说“虚空的虚空，凡事都是虚空”（传 1:2）。“虚空”的希伯来文原意是一口气、一阵雾。作者看到，人在“日光之下”追求的东西都留不住。书的结尾说：“敬畏神，谨守他的诫命，这是人所当尽的本分”（传 12:13）。<br>
**Ecclesiastes** keeps saying, “Vanity of vanities, all is vanity” (Ecclesiastes 1:2). The Hebrew word translated “vanity” literally means a breath or a vapour. The writer sees that everything people pursue “under the sun” slips away. The book ends, “Fear God and keep his commandments; for this is the whole duty of man” (Ecclesiastes 12:13).

三卷书放在一起读比较好。箴言讲通常的规律，约伯记和传道书提醒人，生活里也有规律解释不了的事。<br>
These three books are best read together. Proverbs shows how life usually works, and Job and Ecclesiastes remind us that life also holds things those patterns cannot explain.
