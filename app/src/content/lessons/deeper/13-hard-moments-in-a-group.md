## 先听 | Listen first

雅各说：“你们各人要快快地听，慢慢地说，慢慢地动怒”（雅 1:19）。《箴言》也说：“未曾听完先回答的，便是他的愚昧和羞辱”（箴 18:13）。下面几种情况，第一步都是先听清楚。<br>
James says, “Let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger” (James 1:19). Proverbs says, “He who answers before he hears, that is folly and shame to him” (Proverbs 18:13). In each of the situations below, the first step is to listen well.

## 有人在痛苦中 | When someone is in pain

查经时，有人可能突然说出家里的变故、生病或失去亲人。这时可以把讨论停一停：<br>
During a study, someone may suddenly mention trouble at home, illness, or the loss of someone close. When that happens, you can pause the discussion:

- 谢谢他愿意说出来，问他愿不愿意多讲一点。<br>
  Thank them for sharing and ask whether they would like to say more.
- 不要急着解释原因或给建议。“与哀哭的人要同哭”（罗 12:15）。<br>
  Do not rush to explain why it happened or to give advice. “Weep with those who weep” (Romans 12:15).
- 问他是否愿意大家一起为他祷告。<br>
  Ask whether they would like the group to pray for them.
- 会后私下联系，看有什么实际的需要。<br>
  Follow up privately afterwards to see what practical needs they have.

## 讨论起争执 | When a discussion turns into an argument

组员对一些经文、教义或社会话题看法不同，讨论变得激烈。这时可以：<br>
Members may disagree about a passage, a doctrine, or a social issue, and the discussion may grow heated. You can:

- 先说出双方的看法，让每个人觉得被听见了。<br>
  Restate both views so that everyone feels heard.
- 分清楚：这是信仰的根基问题，还是正统基督徒之间可以有不同看法的问题？<br>
  Clarify whether this is a question about the foundations of the faith or one on which orthodox Christians can differ.
- 把讨论带回经文：“这一段经文本身怎么说？”<br>
  Bring the discussion back to the text: “What does this passage itself say?”
- 和查经主题关系不大的争论，可以说“我们会后再聊”，然后继续。<br>
  If the argument has little to do with the study, say, “Let's talk about this afterwards,” and move on.

保罗说，主的仆人“不可争竞，只要温温和和地待众人”（提后 2:24）。<br>
Paul says the Lord's servant “must not quarrel, but be gentle toward all” (2 Timothy 2:24).

## 有人一直说话，有人一直不说 | When some talk a lot and others say nothing

- 对说得多的人，可以说：“谢谢你的分享，我们也听听其他人怎么想。”也可以会后私下请他帮你一起鼓励别人发言。<br>
  To someone who talks a lot you can say, “Thanks for sharing. Let's hear what others think.” You might also ask them privately afterwards to help you draw others in.
- 对不说话的人，不要点名逼他回答难题。可以问一个容易回答的观察题，或者在小组里分成两三人一组讨论。<br>
  With quiet members, do not put them on the spot with a hard question. Ask an easy observation question, or split the group into pairs or threes for discussion.

保罗说：“要警戒不守规矩的人，勉励灰心的人，扶助软弱的人，也要向众人忍耐”（帖前 5:14）。不同的人需要不同的回应。<br>
Paul writes, “Admonish the disorderly; encourage the faint-hearted; support the weak; be patient toward all” (1 Thessalonians 5:14). Different people need different responses.

## 有人可能有危险 | When someone may be in danger

如果组员说出想伤害自己，或者自己、孩子正在受到暴力或虐待，这已经超出小组能处理的范围：<br>
If a member says they are thinking of harming themselves, or that they or a child are suffering violence or abuse, this is beyond what a small group can handle:

1. **认真对待**，不要轻描淡写。<br>
   **Take it seriously.** Do not brush it aside.
2. **先顾安全**：如果有立即的危险，联系当地的紧急服务。<br>
   **Put safety first**: If there is immediate danger, contact local emergency services.
3. **请合适的人帮助**：告诉教会负责的牧者或同工，并帮助当事人联系专业的辅导或社会服务。<br>
   **Bring in the right people**: Tell the responsible pastor or church leaders, and help the person connect with professional counselling or social services.
4. **说明保密的限度**：小组里的事一般要保密，但涉及人身安全时，需要让能帮助的人知道。<br>
   **Explain the limits of confidentiality**: What is shared in the group is normally kept private, but when someone's safety is at risk, the people who can help need to know.

带小组的人不需要什么都懂、什么都能解决。知道什么时候请别人帮忙，也是带领的一部分。<br>
A group leader does not need to know everything or fix everything. Knowing when to ask for help is part of leading.
