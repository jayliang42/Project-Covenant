## 故事和结构 | The story and its structure

约伯记由散文和诗歌两部分组成：<br>
Job is made up of prose and poetry:

- **序幕（1–2 章，散文）**：约伯“完全正直，敬畏神，远离恶事”（伯 1:8）。撒但说约伯敬畏神是因为得了好处。约伯失去儿女和财产，又长了毒疮。他说：“赏赐的是耶和华，收取的也是耶和华。耶和华的名是应当称颂的”（伯 1:21）。<br>
  **Prologue (chapters 1–2, prose)**: Job is “a blameless and an upright man, one who fears God, and turns away from evil” (Job 1:8). Satan claims Job fears God only because it pays. Job loses his children and property and is struck with sores. He says, “Yahweh gave, and Yahweh has taken away. Blessed be Yahweh's name” (Job 1:21).
- **对话（3–31 章，诗歌）**：约伯和三个朋友三轮辩论，最后是约伯的长篇独白。第 28 章是一首关于智慧的诗。<br>
  **Dialogue (chapters 3–31, poetry)**: Job and his three friends debate in three cycles, ending with Job's long monologue. Chapter 28 is a poem about wisdom.
- **以利户的话（32–37 章）**：一个年轻人插进来讲话。<br>
  **Elihu's speeches (chapters 32–37)**: A young man breaks in to speak.
- **神的回答（38:1–42:6）**：神从旋风中对约伯说话，约伯回应。这是全书的高潮。<br>
  **God's answer (38:1–42:6)**: God speaks to Job from the whirlwind, and Job responds. This is the climax of the book.
- **尾声（42:7-17，散文）**：神责备三个朋友，恢复约伯的家业。<br>
  **Epilogue (42:7-17, prose)**: God rebukes the three friends and restores Job's fortunes.

## 谁写的，什么时候 | Who wrote it, and when

作者不知道是谁。有的犹太传统认为是摩西写的。大英百科介绍的学术看法是：散文的框架可能是一个古老的故事，诗歌的对话部分，多数学者过去认为写于主前 4 世纪，也有越来越多人认为写于主前 6 世纪的被掳时期。不少学者认为以利户的话是后来加进去的；持守传统的学者则认为全书是一个整体。故事的背景像族长时代，约伯不是以色列人。<br>
The author is unknown. Some Jewish tradition attributes the book to Moses. Scholarly views summarised by Britannica are that the prose framework may be an old tale, and that most scholars once dated the poetic dialogue to the fourth century BC, though a growing number place it in the exile of the sixth century BC. Many scholars think the Elihu speeches were added later; scholars holding traditional views see the book as a unity. The setting resembles the patriarchal era, and Job is not an Israelite.

## 朋友们错在哪里 | Where the friends went wrong

以利法、比勒达和琐法的道理很简单：神是公义的，所以好人得福，恶人受祸；约伯受了这样大的苦，一定犯了大罪，只要认罪就会恢复。这种看法在箴言里也能找到相似的话，作为一般的原则并不错。他们的错在于把它当成没有例外的公式，用在约伯身上。<br>
Eliphaz, Bildad, and Zophar reason simply: God is just, so the righteous prosper and the wicked suffer; Job has suffered terribly, so he must have sinned greatly, and if he confesses he will be restored. Similar sayings can be found in Proverbs, and as a general principle this is not wrong. Their mistake was to treat it as a formula without exceptions and apply it to Job.

约伯坚持自己的清白，也大胆地向神发出抱怨和质问。在最痛苦的时候，他仍然说：“我知道我的救赎主活着，末了必站立在地上”（伯 19:25）。<br>
Job maintains his innocence and boldly brings complaints and questions to God. In the depths of his pain he still says, “I know that my Redeemer lives. In the end, he will stand upon the earth” (Job 19:25).

## 神的回答 | God's answer

神没有告诉约伯第 1–2 章里天上发生的事，也没有解释约伯为什么受苦。他问约伯：“我立大地根基的时候，你在哪里呢？”（伯 38:4）接着讲到星辰、海洋、野兽，让约伯看见神的智慧和权能远超过人的理解。<br>
God does not tell Job what happened in heaven in chapters 1–2, nor explain why Job suffered. He asks Job, “Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth?” (Job 38:4), and goes on to speak of the stars, the sea, and wild animals, showing Job that God's wisdom and power far exceed human understanding.

约伯回答：“我从前风闻有你，现在亲眼看见你”（伯 42:5）。约伯没有得到他要的解释。他遇见了神。神还说，约伯议论他比三个朋友说的对（伯 42:7）。约伯的抱怨是向神说的；朋友们的道理是关于神说的，却说错了。<br>
Job answers, “I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you” (Job 42:5). Job did not get the explanation he wanted. He met God. God also says that Job spoke of him more rightly than his friends did (Job 42:7). Job's complaints were spoken to God; the friends' arguments were spoken about God, and were wrong.

## 怎样读这卷书 | How to read the book

- 记住读者知道而约伯不知道的事：约伯受苦不是因为犯罪。<br>
  Remember what readers know and Job does not: his suffering is not because of sin.
- 不要从朋友们的话里随便摘句子当作神的话来引用。要看全书最后神怎样评价他们。<br>
  Don't lift lines from the friends' speeches and quote them as God's word. Look at how God judges them at the end.
- 这卷书没有回答“为什么受苦”的所有问题。它让受苦的人可以对神说真话，也提醒陪伴的人少下结论。“陪伴受苦的人”一课讲到这一点。<br>
  The book leaves some questions about suffering unanswered. It lets sufferers speak truthfully to God, and it reminds companions to be slow to draw conclusions. The lesson on walking with those who suffer takes this up.
