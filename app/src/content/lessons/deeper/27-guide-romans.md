## 写作背景 | Setting

保罗大约在主后 57 年写这封信，地点很可能在哥林多。他准备把外邦教会的捐款送到耶路撒冷，然后路过罗马，再往西班牙传福音（罗 15:24-26）。罗马教会不是他建立的，里面有犹太信徒，也有外邦信徒。主后 49 年前后，罗马皇帝革老丢（克劳狄）曾命犹太人离开罗马（徒 18:2），几年后他们才陆续回来，这可能造成了教会里两群人之间的张力。<br>
Paul wrote this letter about AD 57, most likely from Corinth. He was about to take the Gentile churches' gift to Jerusalem and then hoped to pass through Rome on his way to preach in Spain (Romans 15:24-26). He had not founded the Roman church, which included both Jewish and Gentile believers. Around AD 49 the emperor Claudius expelled the Jews from Rome (Acts 18:2), and they returned gradually some years later. This may have caused tension between the two groups in the church.

## 结构 | Structure

- **1:1-17 问安和主题**：“这福音本是神的大能，要救一切相信的，先是犹太人，后是希腊人……义人必因信得生”（罗 1:16-17）。<br>
  **1:1-17, greeting and theme**: “it is the power of God for salvation for everyone who believes, for the Jew first, and also for the Greek... the righteous shall live by faith” (Romans 1:16-17).
- **1:18–3:20 所有人都需要救恩**：外邦人和犹太人都在罪中，“世人都犯了罪，亏缺了神的荣耀”（罗 3:23）。<br>
  **1:18–3:20, all need salvation**: Gentiles and Jews alike are under sin; “all have sinned, and fall short of the glory of God” (Romans 3:23).
- **3:21–5:21 因信称义**：人“蒙神的恩典，因基督耶稣的救赎，就白白地称义”（罗 3:24）；亚伯拉罕是因信称义的榜样；亚当和基督的对照。<br>
  **3:21–5:21, justification by faith**: people are “justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus” (Romans 3:24); Abraham as the example of faith; Adam and Christ contrasted.
- **6–8 章 在基督里的新生命**：向罪死、向神活；律法和罪的挣扎；“如今，那些在基督耶稣里的就不定罪了”（罗 8:1），以至“无论是死，是生……都不能叫我们与神的爱隔绝”。<br>
  **Chapters 6–8, new life in Christ**: dead to sin and alive to God; the struggle with law and sin; “There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus” (Romans 8:1), and nothing “will be able to separate us from God’s love.”
- **9–11 章 神和以色列**：以色列大多不信，神的应许落空了吗？保罗的回答以赞美结束：“深哉，神丰富的智慧和知识！”（罗 11:33）<br>
  **Chapters 9–11, God and Israel**: Most of Israel does not believe; has God's promise failed? Paul's answer ends in praise: “Oh the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and the knowledge of God!” (Romans 11:33).
- **12–15 章 怎样生活**：“将身体献上，当作活祭”（罗 12:1）；彼此相爱；在饮食和守日上，信心强的和信心弱的彼此接纳。<br>
  **Chapters 12–15, how to live**: “present your bodies a living sacrifice” (Romans 12:1); love one another; the strong and the weak in faith are to welcome each other in matters of food and holy days.
- **16 章 问候**：保罗问候了二十多人，其中有不少妇女，第一位是坚革哩教会的非比，她可能就是带信去罗马的人。<br>
  **Chapter 16, greetings**: Paul greets more than twenty people, a number of them women. The first is Phoebe of the church at Cenchreae, who may have carried the letter to Rome.

## 这封信改变过的人 | People this letter changed

主后 386 年，奥古斯丁在米兰的花园里听见孩童唱“拿起来读”，翻开读到罗马书 13:13-14，从此归信。1515 年前后，路德讲授罗马书，明白了“神的义”是神白白赐给信的人的。1738 年 5 月 24 日，约翰·卫斯理在伦敦奥德斯门街的聚会上，听人读路德的罗马书序言，“心里异样地温暖”。<br>
In AD 386 Augustine, in a garden in Milan, heard a child's voice chanting “take up and read,” opened to Romans 13:13-14, and was converted. Around 1515 Luther, lecturing on Romans, came to understand “the righteousness of God” as God's free gift to those who believe. On 24 May 1738, at a meeting in Aldersgate Street in London, John Wesley heard Luther's preface to Romans read and felt his heart “strangely warmed.”

## 怎样读保罗：新旧两种看法 | How to read Paul: two views

1977 年，学者桑德斯（E. P. Sanders）出版《保罗与巴勒斯坦犹太教》，引起了一场至今未完的讨论。<br>
In 1977 the scholar E. P. Sanders published Paul and Palestinian Judaism, which began a discussion that continues today.

- **传统的读法**（常和路德、加尔文连在一起）：保罗反对的是人想靠遵守律法、积累功德来赢得神的接纳。“称义”回答的是：一个罪人怎样在圣洁的神面前被算为义？答案是唯独因信，基督的义归在信的人身上。<br>
  **The traditional reading** (often linked with Luther and Calvin): Paul opposes the attempt to win God's acceptance by keeping the law and earning merit. Justification answers the question: how can a sinner be counted righteous before a holy God? The answer is by faith alone, with Christ's righteousness credited to the believer.
- **“保罗新观”**（桑德斯、邓雅各、赖特等）：桑德斯认为，当时的犹太教并不教导靠行为得救，而是认为人因神的恩典进入盟约，遵守律法是为了留在盟约里，他称之为“盟约守法主义”。邓雅各认为，保罗说的“律法的行为”主要指割礼、饮食律法和守安息日这些区分犹太人和外邦人的标记。赖特认为，“称义”首先回答的是：谁属于神的百姓？答案是所有信耶稣的人，无论犹太人还是外邦人。<br>
  **The New Perspective on Paul** (Sanders, James Dunn, N. T. Wright, and others): Sanders argued that the Judaism of the time did not teach salvation by works. It held that people enter the covenant by God's grace and keep the law to remain in it, which he called “covenantal nomism.” Dunn argued that by “works of the law” Paul chiefly meant circumcision, food laws, and Sabbath, the markers that separated Jews from Gentiles. Wright argues that justification first answers the question, who belongs to God's people? The answer is all who believe in Jesus, Jew and Gentile alike.

批评新观的学者，例如韦斯特霍姆，指出罗马书 4:4-5 说“做工的得工价，不算恩典……只信称罪人为义的神”，保罗在这里讲的明明是一般的行为和功德。也有很多学者认为两种读法可以互相补充：罗马书既讲罪人怎样被神接纳，也讲犹太人和外邦人怎样在基督里成为一家。<br>
Critics of the New Perspective, such as Stephen Westerholm, point to Romans 4:4-5, “to him who works, the reward is not counted as grace... but believes in him who justifies the ungodly,” where Paul plainly speaks of works and merit in general. Many scholars also hold that the two readings complement each other: Romans speaks both of how sinners are accepted by God and of how Jews and Gentiles become one family in Christ.

这场讨论主要在新教学者之间进行。天主教和东正教对称义有自己的理解，“宗教改革”和“称义和成圣”两课介绍过。<br>
This discussion has taken place mainly among Protestant scholars. Catholics and Orthodox have their own understanding of justification, introduced in the earlier lessons on the Reformation and on justification and sanctification.
