## 五卷的结构 | The five books

《诗篇》150 篇分成五卷，每卷末尾都有一句颂赞，例如“耶和华以色列的神是应当称颂的，从亘古直到永远”（诗 41:13）。<br>
The 150 psalms are divided into five books, each ending with a doxology, such as “Blessed be Yahweh, the God of Israel, from everlasting and to everlasting!” (Psalm 41:13).

| 卷 Book | 篇 Psalms | 特点 Features |
|---|---|---|
| 一 I | 1–41 | 多数署名大卫，个人的哀歌多<br>Mostly titled for David, many personal laments |
| 二 II | 42–72 | 可拉后裔的诗，结尾说“大卫的祈祷完毕”（72:20）<br>Psalms of the sons of Korah; ends “This ends the prayers by David” (72:20) |
| 三 III | 73–89 | 亚萨的诗，多讲国家的灾难，89 篇问神为什么像是废了大卫的约<br>Psalms of Asaph, many on national disaster; Psalm 89 asks why God seems to have renounced the covenant with David |
| 四 IV | 90–106 | 以摩西的祷告开头，多篇宣告“耶和华作王”<br>Opens with a prayer of Moses; several psalms declare “Yahweh reigns” |
| 五 V | 107–150 | 感恩、上行之诗，最后五篇都以“哈利路亚”开头和结束<br>Thanksgiving and the Songs of Ascents; the last five psalms begin and end with “Hallelujah” |

很多学者注意到，这五卷的次序像是在讲一个故事：大卫王朝建立，后来衰败、被掳，以色列人在国破家亡以后重新学习“耶和华作王”，最后全地赞美神。<br>
Many scholars have noticed that the order of the five books seems to tell a story: David's dynasty is established, then declines and goes into exile, and after losing everything Israel learns again that “Yahweh reigns,” and in the end all the earth praises God.

## 开头的两篇 | The first two psalms

第 1 篇和第 2 篇都没有标题，常被看作全书的“门口”。第 1 篇讲个人：喜爱神的律法、“昼夜思想”的人是有福的（诗 1:2）。第 2 篇讲列国和王：列国反对神和他的受膏者，神却说：“你是我的儿子，我今日生你”（诗 2:7）。<br>
Psalms 1 and 2 have no titles and are often seen as the doorway to the whole book. Psalm 1 is about the individual: blessed is the one who delights in God's law and “meditates day and night” on it (Psalm 1:2). Psalm 2 is about the nations and the king: the nations rage against God and his anointed, and God says, “You are my son. Today I have become your father” (Psalm 2:7).

所以读诗篇的人一开始就知道两件事：要用神的话来祷告和生活；神立的王最终要掌权。<br>
So from the start a reader of the Psalms knows two things: pray and live by God's word, and the king God has appointed will reign in the end.

## 新约怎样引用诗篇 | How the New Testament quotes the Psalms

新约引用诗篇的次数比引用其他旧约书卷都多。几篇常被引用的诗：<br>
The New Testament quotes the Psalms more than any other Old Testament book. Some psalms often quoted:

- **第 2 篇**：耶稣受洗和登山变像时，天上的声音和这篇诗相呼应。使徒行传 13:33 和希伯来书 1:5 用它讲耶稣的复活和身份。<br>
  **Psalm 2**: The voice from heaven at Jesus' baptism and transfiguration echoes this psalm. Acts 13:33 and Hebrews 1:5 use it of Jesus' resurrection and identity.
- **第 22 篇**：耶稣在十字架上用它的第一句祷告，福音书描写分他衣服、讥诮他的细节，也和这篇诗相合。这篇诗的结尾说“地的四极都要想念耶和华”（诗 22:27）。<br>
  **Psalm 22**: Jesus prayed its first line on the cross, and the Gospels' details of the dividing of his clothes and the mocking match it. It ends, “All the ends of the earth shall remember and turn to Yahweh” (Psalm 22:27).
- **第 110 篇**：“耶和华对我主说：你坐在我的右边。”这是新约引用最多的旧约经文之一，用来讲耶稣升天以后坐在神的右边。<br>
  **Psalm 110**: “Yahweh says to my Lord, ‘Sit at my right hand.’” It is one of the Old Testament texts most quoted in the New Testament, used of Jesus seated at God's right hand after his ascension.
- **第 118 篇**：“匠人所弃的石头已成了房角的头块石头。”耶稣用它讲自己被弃绝（太 21:42）。<br>
  **Psalm 118**: “The stone which the builders rejected has become the cornerstone.” Jesus uses it of his own rejection (Matthew 21:42).

## 求神报应仇敌的诗 | Psalms that ask God to repay enemies

有些诗篇求神惩罚仇敌，话说得很重。最常被提到的是 137 篇的结尾：“拿你的婴孩摔在磐石上的，那人便为有福”（诗 137:9）。这篇诗是被掳到巴比伦的人写的，他们亲眼见过耶路撒冷被毁。<br>
Some psalms ask God to punish enemies in very strong words. The one most often mentioned is the end of Psalm 137: “Happy shall he be, who takes and dashes your little ones against the rock” (Psalm 137:9). It was written by exiles in Babylon who had seen Jerusalem destroyed.

信徒对这类诗有几种理解，它们常常同时被采用：<br>
Believers understand such psalms in several ways, often holding more than one at once:

- **诚实的祷告**：作者没有自己报仇，而是把愤怒交给神。这正好符合“伸冤在我”的教导。圣经让受害的人可以把最黑暗的感受说出来。<br>
  **Prayer that holds nothing back**: The writers do not take revenge themselves. They hand their anger to God, which fits the teaching “vengeance belongs to me.” Scripture lets victims voice their darkest feelings.
- **呼求公义**：这些诗求的是神在恶面前施行公义，和启示录里殉道者问“要等到几时”（启 6:10）一致。<br>
  **A cry for justice**: These psalms ask God to act justly against evil, in line with the martyrs in Revelation who ask “How long?” (Revelation 6:10).
- **要用耶稣的教导来读**：耶稣教导门徒爱仇敌、为逼迫他们的人祷告。很多信徒认为，基督徒可以借这些诗学会诚实地祷告，却不能把其中的咒诅当作自己对人的愿望。<br>
  **To be read in the light of Jesus' teaching**: Jesus taught his disciples to love their enemies and pray for their persecutors. Many believers hold that Christians can learn from these psalms to pray with candour without taking their curses as their own wishes toward others.

礼仪传统的教会在公祷中如何使用这些经文，做法也不同。梵二会议以后修订的天主教《时辰祈祷》，按教宗保禄六世的决定，略去了三篇诗和另外十九篇诗里的部分经节，约 120 节。东正教和很多新教教会在诵读时保留全部诗篇。<br>
Liturgical churches differ in how they use these texts in public prayer. In the Catholic Liturgy of the Hours revised after the Second Vatican Council, by decision of Pope Paul VI, three whole psalms and parts of nineteen others, about 120 verses in all, are left out. The Orthodox and many Protestant churches keep the whole Psalter in their reading.
