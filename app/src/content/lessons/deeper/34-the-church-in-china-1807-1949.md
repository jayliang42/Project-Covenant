## 开始：马礼逊和最早的信徒 | Beginnings: Morrison and the first believers

1807 年，伦敦会的马礼逊来到广州，是第一位来华的新教宣教士。当时清政府禁止外国人传教，他只能以东印度公司翻译的身份留下，私下翻译圣经、编写字典。<br>
In 1807 Robert Morrison of the London Missionary Society arrived in Guangzhou, the first Protestant missionary to China. The Qing government then forbade foreigners to preach, so he stayed on as a translator for the East India Company and worked quietly on the Bible and a dictionary.

七年以后，1814 年 7 月 16 日，帮他做印刷工作的蔡高在澳门受洗，成为第一位中国新教信徒。1816 年，另一位印刷工人梁发由米怜施洗。梁发后来成为第一位中国籍的新教传道人，写了一本介绍基督教的书《劝世良言》。1830 年代，一位落第的广东书生洪秀全读到了这本书，后来他发动太平天国运动，宣扬一套混杂的宗教主张，宣教士和中国信徒大多不承认那是基督教的信仰。<br>
Seven years later, on 16 July 1814, Cai Gao, who helped with the printing, was baptized in Macao, the first Chinese Protestant. In 1816 another printer, Liang Fa, was baptized by William Milne. Liang later became the first Chinese Protestant evangelist and wrote a book introducing Christianity, Good Words to Admonish the Age. In the 1830s Hong Xiuquan, a Guangdong scholar who had failed the civil examinations, read this book. He later led the Taiping movement and preached a mixed set of religious ideas that most missionaries and Chinese Christians did not recognise as the Christian faith.

## 条约以后 | After the treaties

两次鸦片战争以后，清政府在列强压力下签订的条约开放了通商口岸，后来又准许传教士进入内地。宣教士人数大大增加，1865 年戴德生创立了中国内地会。各差会办了很多学校、医院、孤儿院和出版机构，例如后来的齐鲁大学、燕京大学、圣约翰大学等。<br>
After the two Opium Wars, treaties signed by the Qing government under pressure from the Western powers opened treaty ports and later allowed missionaries into the interior. The number of missionaries grew greatly, and in 1865 Hudson Taylor founded the China Inland Mission. The missions founded many schools, hospitals, orphanages, and publishing houses, among them the institutions that became Cheeloo University, Yenching University, and St. John's University.

传教和不平等条约连在一起，在中国社会引起了很深的不满，各地发生了多次“教案”，就是民众和教会之间的冲突。<br>
Because missions were tied to the unequal treaties, they caused deep resentment in Chinese society, and many “missionary cases,” conflicts between local people and churches, broke out across the country.

## 1900 年 | The year 1900

1900 年的义和团运动中，大批外国宣教士和中国信徒被杀。各方的统计数字不同：大英百科说外国人约有两百到两百五十人被杀，多数是宣教士，中国信徒被杀的有“数千人”；也有资料估计中国信徒的死难人数在三万人左右。内地会一家就有 58 位宣教士和 21 个孩子遇难。<br>
In the Boxer Uprising of 1900 large numbers of foreign missionaries and Chinese Christians were killed. The figures vary by source. Britannica says some 200 to 250 foreigners, most of them missionaries, were killed, along with “thousands” of Chinese Christians; other sources estimate around 30,000 Chinese Christian deaths. The China Inland Mission alone lost 58 missionaries and 21 children.

天主教会后来把其中一部分殉道者封为圣人，2000 年封圣的“中华殉道圣人”一百二十位中，大部分死于 1900 年。<br>
The Catholic Church later canonised some of these martyrs. Of the 120 Martyr Saints of China canonised in 2000, most died in 1900.

## 中国信徒自己的教会 | Chinese believers' own churches

20 世纪初，越来越多的中国信徒希望教会不再依赖外国差会。20 世纪 20 年代，中国信徒越来越多地谈到教会要“自立、自养、自传”。这三个说法最早出自 19 世纪西方的宣教理论，中国信徒用它来表达建立本色教会的心愿。<br>
In the early 20th century more and more Chinese believers wanted the church to stop depending on foreign missions. In the 1920s Chinese Christians spoke more and more of a church that would be self-governing, self-supporting, and self-propagating. The three terms came from 19th-century Western mission theory, and Chinese believers used them to express their hope for an indigenous church.

这一时期出现了好几个由中国信徒建立、不属于任何外国宗派的教会：<br>
In this period several churches arose that were founded by Chinese believers and belonged to no foreign denomination:

- **真耶稣教会**：1917 年由魏保罗在北京创立，守星期六为安息日，重视说方言和医病。<br>
  **The True Jesus Church**: founded by Paul Wei in Beijing in 1917. It keeps Saturday as the Sabbath and stresses speaking in tongues and healing.
- **耶稣家庭**：1926 年由敬奠瀛在山东开始，信徒同住、共有财产。<br>
  **The Jesus Family**: begun by Jing Dianying in Shandong in 1926. Its members lived together and held property in common.
- **“小群”或地方教会**：倪柝声 1928 年在上海建立聚会，主张一地一会，不设宗派和按立的牧师，受英国弟兄会影响很大。<br>
  **The “Little Flock” or local churches**: Watchman Nee started a congregation in Shanghai in 1928. He taught one church for each locality, with no denominations or ordained clergy, and was strongly influenced by the British Brethren.
- **基督徒会堂**：王明道在北京独立传道，1937 年建成会堂，以讲台严谨、生活严格著称。<br>
  **The Christian Tabernacle**: Wang Mingdao preached independently in Beijing and completed his tabernacle in 1937. He was known for rigorous preaching and strict living.

1927 年以后，宋尚节在中国和东南亚各地举行布道会，很多人在他的聚会中悔改信主。他 1944 年去世。<br>
From 1927 John Sung held evangelistic meetings across China and Southeast Asia, and many people repented and believed in his meetings. He died in 1944.

这些人和教会在神学上并不一样，彼此也有分歧，例如对安息日、灵恩和教会组织的看法。他们的共同点是：由中国人带领，用中国人能明白的方式传讲圣经。<br>
These leaders and churches did not share a single theology and disagreed with one another, for example on the Sabbath, charismatic gifts, and church order. What they had in common was that they were led by Chinese people and preached the Bible in ways Chinese people could understand.

## 天主教的情况 | The Catholic Church

同一时期，天主教在中国也有很大的发展。1926 年，教宗庇护十一世在罗马亲自祝圣了六位中国籍主教，这是近代第一批中国籍主教。1939 年，教廷准许中国信徒参加敬孔祭祖的礼仪，结束了延续两百多年的“礼仪之争”。<br>
In the same period the Catholic Church in China also grew substantially. In 1926 Pope Pius XI personally consecrated six Chinese bishops in Rome, the first Chinese bishops of modern times. In 1939 the Holy See permitted Chinese Catholics to take part in rites honouring Confucius and ancestors, ending the Rites Controversy that had lasted more than two centuries.

## 1949 年 | 1949

到 1949 年，中国大约有一百万新教徒和三百万天主教徒，在四亿多人口中只占很小的比例。本课只讲到这一年。这以后的历史需要另外的资料和更谨慎的处理，这里不讨论。<br>
By 1949 China had about a million Protestants and three million Catholics, a small fraction of a population of more than four hundred million. This lesson ends at that year. The history after it needs other sources and more careful handling and is not discussed here.

一百四十多年里，福音在中国经历了很多艰难。耶稣说：“一粒麦子不落在地里死了，仍旧是一粒，若是死了，就结出许多子粒来”（约 12:24）。<br>
Over these hundred and forty years the gospel in China passed through great difficulty. Jesus said, “unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains by itself alone. But if it dies, it bears much fruit” (John 12:24).
