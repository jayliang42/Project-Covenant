## 大家都同意的地方 | What all agree on

各传统都相信“圣徒相通”：所有属于基督的人，不论活着的还是已经离世的，都在基督里联合在一起。耶稣说：“神原不是死人的神，乃是活人的神；因为在他那里，人都是活的”（路 20:38）。希伯来书说，我们“有这许多的见证人，如同云彩围着我们”（来 12:1）。<br>
All traditions believe in the “communion of saints”: all who belong to Christ, living and departed, are united in him. Jesus said, “he is not the God of the dead, but of the living, for all are alive to him” (Luke 20:38). Hebrews says we are “surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses” (Hebrews 12:1).

各传统也都鼓励信徒彼此代求：“要彼此认罪，互相代求”（雅 5:16）；“为万人恳求、祷告、代求”（提前 2:1）。各传统都认为，敬拜只归给神。<br>
All traditions also encourage believers to pray for one another: “Confess your offenses to one another, and pray for one another” (James 5:16); “petitions, prayers, intercessions... be made for all men” (1 Timothy 2:1). All hold that worship belongs to God alone.

分歧在于：能不能请已经离世的圣徒为我们代求？能不能在敬拜中使用圣像？<br>
The disagreements are these: may we ask departed saints to pray for us? May images be used in worship?

## 请圣徒代求 | Asking the saints to intercede

- **天主教**：《天主教教理》第 956 条说，天上的圣徒和基督更紧密地联合，“不停地为我们在父面前代求”。天主教区分对神的“朝拜”和对圣徒的“尊敬”。<br>
  **Catholic**: Paragraph 956 of the Catechism says that the saints in heaven, “being more closely united to Christ,” “do not cease to intercede with the Father for us.” Catholic teaching distinguishes the adoration due to God from the honour given to saints.
- **东正教**：同样请圣徒和圣母为信徒代求，礼仪中常有这样的祷文。<br>
  **Orthodox**: Likewise asks the saints and the Mother of God to intercede, and such prayers are frequent in the liturgy.
- **信义宗和大多数新教**：《奥格斯堡信条》第 21 条说，可以纪念圣徒，“使我们效法他们的信心和善行”；但“圣经并没有教导人向圣徒祈求或求圣徒帮助”，因为圣经只把基督立为“中保、挽回祭、大祭司和代求者”。新教徒请活着的弟兄姊妹为自己祷告，但不向已经离世的人祈求，认为“在神和人中间，只有一位中保，乃是降世为人的基督耶稣”（提前 2:5）。<br>
  **Lutheran and most Protestants**: Article 21 of the Augsburg Confession says that the memory of saints may be set before us “that we may follow their faith and good works,” but “the Scripture teaches not the invocation of saints or to ask help of saints,” since it sets before us “the one Christ as the Mediator, Propitiation, High Priest, and Intercessor.” Protestants ask living fellow believers to pray for them but do not pray to the departed, holding that “there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus” (1 Timothy 2:5).

天主教和东正教会回应说：请圣徒代求，就像请活着的弟兄姊妹为自己祷告一样，并不取代基督唯一的中保地位；启示录也描写天上的长老拿着盛满了香的金炉，“这香就是众圣徒的祈祷”（启 5:8）。新教徒则认为，圣经没有说已经离世的人能听见我们的祈求，也没有教导我们这样做。<br>
Catholics and Orthodox respond that asking the saints to intercede is like asking living fellow believers to pray, and does not replace Christ's unique mediation; Revelation pictures the elders in heaven holding golden bowls of incense, “which are the prayers of the saints” (Revelation 5:8). Protestants answer that Scripture does not say the departed can hear our requests, nor teach us to make them.

## 圣像 | Icons and images

8 世纪，拜占庭皇帝利奥三世在 726 年下令反对圣像，引起了长时间的“圣像破坏之争”。787 年的第二次尼西亚会议认为，圣像应当受到“尊敬和敬礼”，但“朝拜”只归给神。东正教每年在大斋期的第一个主日庆祝“正统信仰主日”，纪念恢复对圣像的敬礼。<br>
In 726 the Byzantine emperor Leo III issued a decree against icons, setting off the long Iconoclastic Controversy. The Second Council of Nicaea in 787 held that icons merit “reverence and veneration but not adoration, which is reserved for God.” Each year, on the first Sunday of Great Lent, the Orthodox Church celebrates the Sunday of Orthodoxy, commemorating the restoration of the veneration of icons.

支持圣像的一方常说：神的儿子成了肉身，是“那不能看见之神的像”（西 1:15），所以可以画出基督；对圣像的尊敬归给它所代表的那一位。<br>
Supporters of icons often argue that since the Son of God became flesh and is “the image of the invisible God” (Colossians 1:15), Christ can be depicted, and the honour given to an icon passes to the one it represents.

新教内部看法也不一样。信义宗和圣公会通常保留十字架、彩色玻璃和图画，但不向它们祈求。改革宗传统按十诫的第二条“不可为自己雕刻偶像……不可跪拜那些像”（出 20:4-5），很多教会在敬拜的地方不放画像，有的连十字架也不放。<br>
Protestants differ among themselves. Lutherans and Anglicans usually keep crosses, stained glass, and pictures, but do not pray to them. The Reformed tradition, following the second commandment, “You shall not make for yourselves an idol, nor any image... you shall not bow yourself down to them” (Exodus 20:4-5), often keeps images out of places of worship, and some churches have no cross either.

## 彼此尊重 | Mutual respect

在这些事上各传统的立场差别很大，也都有自己的经文和历史理由。了解别人的理由，同时可以保留自己的立场。这样做能避免误会，例如以为天主教徒“拜马利亚像”就是把她当神，或者以为新教徒不尊重历代的圣徒。<br>
The traditions differ widely on these matters, and each has its own biblical and historical reasons. One can understand others' reasons and keep one's own position. Understanding them can prevent misunderstandings, such as assuming that Catholics who kneel before an image of Mary treat her as God, or that Protestants have no respect for the saints of past ages.
