## 少数人的信仰 | A minority faith

使徒以后的三百年里，基督徒在罗马帝国是少数。他们不拜罗马的神明，也不向皇帝的像献祭，常被人怀疑不忠于国家，甚至被说成“无神论者”。逼迫并不是一直都有，也不是到处都有；有时平静，有时在某些地方非常严酷。“早期教会和信经”一课讲到，主后 64 年尼禄把罗马大火归罪于基督徒；313 年的《米兰敕令》以后，基督徒才可以公开敬拜。<br>
For three centuries after the apostles, Christians were a minority in the Roman Empire. They did not worship Rome's gods or sacrifice before the emperor's image, so they were often suspected of disloyalty and even called “atheists.” Persecution was neither constant nor everywhere; at times things were quiet, and at times in some places it was very severe. As the lesson on the early church and the creeds notes, Nero blamed the Christians for the great fire of Rome in AD 64, and only after the Edict of Milan in 313 could Christians worship openly.

彼得写信给散居各地的信徒说：“你们是客旅，是寄居的……你们在外邦人中，应当品行端正，叫那些毁谤你们是作恶的，因看见你们的好行为，便……归荣耀给神”（彼前 2:11-12）。<br>
Peter wrote to scattered believers, “I beg you as foreigners and pilgrims... having good behavior among the nations, so in that of which they speak against you as evildoers, they may by your good works, which they see, glorify God” (1 Peter 2:11-12).

## 一位罗马总督的报告 | A Roman governor's report

大约主后 112 年，罗马总督小普林尼写信给皇帝图拉真，请教怎样处理被告发的基督徒。他审问以后报告说，基督徒“在固定的日子天亮以前”聚会，“唱诗赞美基督如同赞美神”，并且立誓不欺诈、不偷盗、不奸淫。对于坚持不肯放弃信仰的人，普林尼说他下令把他们处死。<br>
Around AD 112 the Roman governor Pliny the Younger wrote to Emperor Trajan asking how to deal with Christians who had been accused. After questioning them he reported that they met “on a fixed day before dawn,” sang a hymn to Christ “as to a god,” and bound themselves by oath not to commit fraud, theft, or adultery. Of those who persisted in their faith, he wrote that he ordered them executed.

图拉真回信说，基督徒“不应被搜捕”，匿名的指控也不应在审判中采用；但被告发而不肯放弃信仰的，仍要受罚。<br>
Trajan replied that Christians “are not to be sought out” and that anonymous accusations should have no place in a prosecution; yet those accused who would not recant were still to be punished.

## 殉道者 | Martyrs

主后 203 年 3 月 7 日，年轻的母亲柏培都亚（Perpetua）和怀孕的女奴斐丽西达（Felicity）在北非的迦太基殉道。柏培都亚在狱中写了日记，记下她受审和坐监的经过；殉道以前，她把日记交给另一位信徒，那人把后来的事接着写完。这份记录后来称为《柏培都亚和斐丽西达殉道记》。<br>
On 7 March 203 Perpetua, a young mother, and Felicity, a pregnant slave, were martyred at Carthage in North Africa. Perpetua kept a diary in prison recording her trial and imprisonment; before her death she gave it to another believer, who completed the account. The record became The Passion of Saints Perpetua and Felicity.

彼得说：“有火炼的试验临到你们，不要以为奇怪……你们若为基督的名受辱骂，便是有福的”（彼前 4:12-14）。<br>
Peter wrote, “don't be astonished at the fiery trial which has come upon you... If you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are blessed” (1 Peter 4:12-14).

## 他们怎样生活 | How they lived

2 世纪左右写成的《致丢格那妥书》这样描写基督徒：他们和别人“在国家、语言、习俗上”没有分别；“以每一个异乡为本乡，以每一个本乡为异乡”；“他们和众人一样结婚生子，但不丢弃自己的婴孩”；“他们贫穷，却使许多人富足”。<br>
The Epistle to Diognetus, probably written in the second century, describes Christians this way: they are distinguished from others “neither by country, nor language, nor the customs which they observe”; “every foreign land is to them as their native country, and every land of their birth as a land of strangers”; “they marry, as do all; they beget children; but they do not destroy their offspring”; “they are poor, yet make many rich.”

大约主后 362 年，罗马皇帝尤利安想恢复罗马的传统宗教。他在给一位祭司的信里抱怨说，基督徒对陌生人的善待和对死者的照顾，帮助了他们的信仰传开；又说“不敬的加利利人（指基督徒）不但养活他们自己的穷人，也养活我们的穷人”，而“我们的人”却得不到我们的帮助。<br>
Around AD 362 the emperor Julian, who sought to restore Rome's traditional religion, complained in a letter to a priest that the Christians' charity to strangers and care for the graves of the dead had helped spread their faith. He wrote that “the impious Galileans [Christians] feed not only their own poor but ours as well,” while “our people are seen to lack any help from us.”

## 留给今天的 | What it leaves for today

早期基督徒的经历提醒我们：信仰可能要付代价；福音在没有权势支持的时候也能传开；而让人看见的，常常是信徒的生活和爱心。希伯来书的读者曾经“被毁谤，遭患难”，同时“陪伴那些受这样苦难的人”（来 10:33）。<br>
The early Christians' experience reminds us that faith may be costly, that the gospel can spread without the backing of power, and that what people notice is often believers' lives and love. The readers of Hebrews had been “exposed to both reproaches and oppressions,” and at the same time had become “partakers with those who were treated so” (Hebrews 10:33).
