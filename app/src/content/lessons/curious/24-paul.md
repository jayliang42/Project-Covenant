## 逼迫教会的人 | A persecutor of the church

保罗原名扫罗，生在大数（今天土耳其南部），是犹太人，属于法利赛派，熟读律法。他自己说：“我从前在犹太教中……怎样极力逼迫残害神的教会。我又……为我祖宗的遗传更加热心”（加 1:13-14）。司提反殉道的时候，他在旁边看守众人的衣服（徒 7:58）。<br>
Paul, first called Saul, was born in Tarsus, in what is now southern Turkey. He was a Jew, a Pharisee, and well trained in the law. He says of himself, “I persecuted the assembly of God and ravaged it... being more exceedingly zealous for the traditions of my fathers” (Galatians 1:13-14). When Stephen was martyred, he stood by, keeping watch over the clothes of those who stoned him (Acts 7:58).

## 大马士革路上 | On the road to Damascus

大约主后 33 年，他拿着大祭司的文书去大马士革捉拿基督徒。路上，忽然有光从天上照着他，他仆倒在地，听见有声音说：“扫罗！扫罗！你为什么逼迫我？”他问：“主啊！你是谁？”那声音说：“我就是你所逼迫的耶稣”（徒 9:4-5）。<br>
Around AD 33 he set out for Damascus with letters from the high priest to arrest Christians. On the way a light from the sky suddenly shone around him. He fell to the ground and heard a voice: “Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?” He asked, “Who are you, Lord?” The voice said, “I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting” (Acts 9:4-5).

他瞎了三天，后来一位叫亚拿尼亚的信徒为他按手，他就看得见了，并且受了洗。主对亚拿尼亚说，这人“是我所拣选的器皿，要在外邦人和君王，并以色列人面前宣扬我的名”（徒 9:15）。<br>
He was blind for three days. Then a believer named Ananias laid hands on him, his sight returned, and he was baptized. The Lord had told Ananias that this man “is my chosen vessel to bear my name before the nations and kings, and the children of Israel” (Acts 9:15).

## 外邦人的使徒 | Apostle to the Gentiles

主后 40 年代和 50 年代，保罗几次长途旅行，走遍今天的土耳其和希腊，在许多城市建立了教会，例如加拉太、腓立比、帖撒罗尼迦、哥林多和以弗所。他写信给这些教会，回答他们的问题，劝勉他们。这些信后来成了新约的一部分。<br>
In the AD 40s and 50s Paul made several long journeys through what are now Turkey and Greece, founding churches in many cities, such as those of Galatia, Philippi, Thessalonica, Corinth, and Ephesus. He wrote letters to these churches, answering their questions and encouraging them. These letters later became part of the New Testament.

他为此付出很大的代价。他列举自己的经历：“被犹太人鞭打五次，每次四十减去一下；被棍打了三次；被石头打了一次；遇着船坏三次”（林后 11:24-25）。后来他在耶路撒冷被捕，被押送到罗马。按教会的传统，他在主后 60 年代尼禄皇帝在位的时候，在罗马殉道。<br>
It cost him a great deal. He lists what he endured: “Five times I received forty stripes minus one from the Jews. Three times I was beaten with rods. Once I was stoned. Three times I suffered shipwreck” (2 Corinthians 11:24-25). Later he was arrested in Jerusalem and taken to Rome under guard. According to church tradition he was martyred in Rome in the AD 60s, during the reign of the emperor Nero.

## 他的信 | His letters

新约 27 卷书里，有 13 卷署名保罗，从《罗马书》到《腓利门书》。学者普遍认为，其中 7 卷出自保罗本人：罗马书、哥林多前后书、加拉太书、腓立比书、帖撒罗尼迦前书、腓利门书。其余 6 卷是不是保罗亲笔，学者看法不一，教会的传统认为都是保罗写的。<br>
Of the New Testament's 27 books, 13 bear Paul's name, from Romans to Philemon. Scholars widely agree that seven come from Paul himself: Romans, 1 and 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, and Philemon. Scholars differ on whether Paul wrote the other six himself, and church tradition attributes all of them to him.

这些信讲的中心是：人得救是因为神的恩典，借着信耶稣，不靠遵守律法；犹太人和外邦人在基督里成为一家。保罗把自己的经历说成一句话：“我已经与基督同钉十字架，现在活着的不再是我，乃是基督在我里面活着”（加 2:20）。<br>
At the centre of these letters is this: people are saved by God's grace through faith in Jesus, not by keeping the law, and Jews and Gentiles become one family in Christ. Paul sums up his own story in one sentence: “I have been crucified with Christ, and it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me” (Galatians 2:20).

## 一个人的转变 | One man's change

保罗从逼迫基督徒的人变成为基督受苦的人。他自己不把这归功于自己：“我原是使徒中最小的，不配称为使徒，因为我从前逼迫神的教会。然而，我今日成了何等人，是蒙神的恩才成的”（林前 15:9-10）。<br>
Paul went from persecuting Christians to suffering for Christ. He took no credit for the change: “I am the least of the apostles, who is not worthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the assembly of God. But by the grace of God I am what I am” (1 Corinthians 15:9-10).

很多读者觉得，保罗的转变本身就是需要解释的事。一个熟读律法、地位稳固、全力反对基督徒的人，为什么会放弃一切，用余生传讲他原来反对的信仰？他自己的回答是：他遇见了复活的耶稣。<br>
Many readers find Paul's change to be something that itself needs explaining. Why would a man learned in the law, secure in his standing, and wholly set against Christians give up everything and spend the rest of his life preaching the faith he had opposed? His own answer was that he met the risen Jesus.
