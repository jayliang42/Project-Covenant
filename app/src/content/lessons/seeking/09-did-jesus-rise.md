## 历史学家怎样提问 | How historians ask the question

历史学家不能做实验来重复过去的事。他们看的是文献，问的是：这份材料有多早？离事件有多近？有几个独立的来源？有没有对作者不利、他却仍然记下来的内容？<br>
Historians cannot repeat the past in an experiment. They work with documents and ask: How early is this source? How close is it to the events? Are there several independent sources? Does it record things that did not help the author's case?

用这些方法看耶稣复活的记载，许多学者，包括一些不信基督教的学者，都接受下面几件事是有历史根据的。<br>
When these methods are applied to the resurrection accounts, many scholars, including some who are not Christians, accept that the following have a historical basis.

## 学者普遍接受的几件事 | Facts most scholars accept

1. **耶稣被钉死在十字架上**：福音书和保罗书信都记载了这件事。罗马史学家塔西佗也提到，基督在彼拉多任内被处死。<br>
   **Jesus died by crucifixion**: The Gospels and Paul's letters record it. The Roman historian Tacitus also mentions that Christ was executed under Pilate.
2. **很早就有人宣讲他复活了**：保罗在《哥林多前书》15:3-7 引用了一段他“所领受”的传统，列出复活的耶稣向谁显现。这封信大约写于主后 50 年代中期，许多学者认为这段传统在耶稣死后几年内就形成了。<br>
   **His resurrection was proclaimed very early**: In 1 Corinthians 15:3–7 Paul quotes a tradition he “received,” listing those to whom the risen Jesus appeared. The letter was written in the mid-50s AD, and many scholars think the tradition took shape within a few years of Jesus' death.
3. **门徒真诚地相信自己见到了复活的耶稣**：他们原来四散逃走，后来却公开宣讲，有些人为此受苦甚至被杀。人会为自己相信的谎言而死，却很少会为自己明知是编出来的事去死。<br>
   **The disciples sincerely believed they had seen the risen Jesus**: They had scattered in fear, yet later preached openly, and some suffered and died for it. People die for lies they believe, but few die for something they know they made up.
4. **原来不信的人也改变了**：耶稣的弟弟雅各起初不信他（约 7:5），后来成了耶路撒冷教会的领袖。逼迫教会的扫罗成了使徒保罗。保罗说，复活的耶稣向雅各显现过，也向他自己显现过（林前 15:7-8）。<br>
   **Former sceptics changed**: Jesus' brother James did not believe in him at first (John 7:5). Later he led the church in Jerusalem. Saul, who persecuted the church, became the apostle Paul. Paul says the risen Jesus appeared to James and also to Paul himself (1 Corinthians 15:7–8).

另外，四福音书都说最先发现空坟墓的是妇女。在当时，妇女的证词常常不被看重。如果故事是编的，作者大概不会这样安排。坟墓是空的这一点，学者之间的争议比上面几点多一些，但也有不少学者接受。<br>
In addition, all four Gospels say that women were the first to find the tomb empty. At the time, women's testimony was often given little weight. If the story were invented, the authors would probably not have told it this way. Scholars debate the empty tomb more than the points above, but many accept it.

## 几种其他解释 | Some other explanations

对这些事，人们提出过几种不同的解释：<br>
People have offered several other explanations for these facts:

- **门徒偷走了尸体**：《马太福音》说当时就有这个说法（太 28:12-15）。可是它很难解释门徒为什么愿意为一个自己编的故事受苦。<br>
  **The disciples stole the body**: Matthew says this story was already going around (Matthew 28:12–15). But it struggles to explain why the disciples would suffer for a story they knew they had made up.
- **耶稣没有真的死**：罗马士兵执行死刑很有经验。一个受过鞭打和十字架刑、勉强活下来的人，也很难让门徒相信他已经胜过了死亡。<br>
  **Jesus did not really die**: Roman soldiers were experienced executioners. And a man who barely survived flogging and crucifixion would hardly convince his followers that he had conquered death.
- **门徒看见的是幻觉**：幻觉通常是个人的经历。记载里有许多人在不同时间、不同地点一起看见，还有原来不信的雅各和保罗。<br>
  **The disciples had hallucinations**: Hallucinations are usually individual experiences. The accounts describe groups of people seeing him at different times and places, and they include sceptics like James and Paul.

## 历史研究能走多远 | How far history can go

历史研究可以说明，最早的门徒宣讲耶稣复活，并且真诚地相信这件事。“神使耶稣从死里复活”这个结论，已经超出了历史学方法本身能判断的范围。一个人是否接受它，和他是否相信有神可以在历史中行事有关。<br>
Historical study can show that the earliest disciples proclaimed Jesus' resurrection and sincerely believed it. The conclusion that “God raised Jesus from the dead” goes beyond what historical method alone can decide. Whether someone accepts it depends partly on whether they think God can act in history.

保罗在雅典讲道时，把复活看作神给万人的凭据（徒 17:31）。基督徒邀请人做的，是像对待其他重要问题一样，认真查看这些证据，再作出自己的判断。<br>
Preaching in Athens, Paul treated the resurrection as God's assurance to all people (Acts 17:31). What Christians invite people to do is to look at this evidence as carefully as they would at any other important question, and then make up their own minds.
