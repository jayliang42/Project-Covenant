## 两种“自由” | Two meanings of freedom

人说“自由”，常常指没有人管我，我想做什么就做什么。这是一种意思。<br>
When people say “freedom,” they often mean that no one tells me what to do and I do as I like. That is one meaning.

还有另一种意思：我有能力去做我应该做、也愿意做的事。一个人想戒烟却戒不掉，没有人拦着他抽烟，可他并不自由。一个钢琴家练了很多年，受了很多约束，才有了在琴键上随意弹奏的自由。鱼离开水就没有了限制，同时也活不成。<br>
There is another meaning: I am able to do what I ought to do and want to do. A person who wants to stop smoking and cannot is not free, though no one prevents him from smoking. A pianist submits to years of disciplined practice and so gains the freedom to play whatever she chooses. A fish out of water has no restrictions, and it cannot live.

所以，光看有没有约束，还看不出一个人自由不自由。要问的是：人按什么方式生活才活得成？<br>
So the presence of limits does not by itself show whether a person is free. The question to ask is which way of living lets a person live at all.

## 耶稣说的自由 | The freedom Jesus speaks of

耶稣说：“你们必晓得真理，真理必叫你们得以自由”（约 8:32）。听的人不服气，说我们从来没有作过谁的奴仆。耶稣回答：“所有犯罪的就是罪的奴仆……天父的儿子若叫你们自由，你们就真自由了”（约 8:34，36）。<br>
Jesus says, “You will know the truth, and the truth will make you free” (John 8:32). His hearers object that they have never been slaves to anyone. Jesus answers, “everyone who commits sin is the bondservant of sin... If therefore the Son makes you free, you will be free indeed” (John 8:34, 36).

按耶稣的说法，人原来并不是站在一块中立的空地上，可以自由地选择信或不信。人已经在听从某些东西：欲望、别人的眼光、对失败的恐惧、对钱的依赖。保罗说得更直接：“你们献上自己作奴仆，顺从谁，就作谁的奴仆”（罗 6:16）。<br>
On Jesus' account people do not stand on neutral ground, free to choose belief or unbelief. They are already obeying something: desire, the opinion of others, the fear of failure, reliance on money. Paul is more direct: “when you present yourselves as servants and obey someone, you are the servants of whomever you obey” (Romans 6:16).

基督信仰说，耶稣把人从这些东西的辖制里释放出来。“基督释放了我们，叫我们得以自由”（加 5:1）。<br>
The Christian faith says that Jesus releases people from the grip of these things. “Stand firm therefore in the liberty by which Christ has made us free” (Galatians 5:1).

## 那么信徒要放下什么 | What, then, does a believer lay down?

跟随耶稣确实要放下一些东西。耶稣没有隐瞒这一点，后面有一课专门讲这个代价。要放下的主要是两样。<br>
Following Jesus does mean laying some things down. Jesus did not hide this, and a later lesson is given to the cost. Two things above all are laid down.

第一是“我说了算”。信的人承认耶稣是主，在道德上不再自己定标准。圣经明确禁止的事，例如撒谎、偷窃、淫乱、仇恨，信徒要离开。<br>
The first is being one's own final authority. A believer confesses Jesus as Lord and no longer sets the moral standard personally. What the Bible plainly forbids, such as lying, theft, sexual immorality, and hatred, the believer leaves behind.

第二是靠表现证明自己。这一点很多人没有想到。信的人不用再靠成绩、收入或别人的称赞来确定自己的价值，因为神已经接纳了他。很多信徒说，这是他们感到最轻松的地方。<br>
The second is proving oneself by performance. Many people do not expect this one. A believer no longer has to establish personal worth through grades, income, or praise, because God has already accepted them. Many believers say this is where they feel the greatest relief.

也有很多事圣经没有规定：吃什么，穿什么，做什么工作，有什么爱好。在这些事上信徒是自由的。不同的教会在一些具体做法上要求不一样，例如喝酒。有的教会要求信徒完全不喝，有的教会只禁止醉酒。遇到这类事，可以问清楚哪些是圣经的要求，哪些是这间教会的习惯。<br>
There are also many things the Bible does not regulate: what to eat, what to wear, what work to do, what hobbies to have. In these a believer is free. Churches differ on some practical matters, such as alcohol. Some ask believers to abstain entirely, and others forbid only drunkenness. In matters like this, it is fair to ask which requirements come from the Bible and which are the custom of that church.

## 一个“轭”的比喻 | The picture of a yoke

耶稣说：“凡劳苦担重担的人可以到我这里来，我就使你们得安息……因为我的轭是容易的，我的担子是轻省的”（太 11:28，30）。<br>
Jesus says, “Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest... For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light” (Matthew 11:28, 30).

轭是套在牲口肩上拉车的木具。耶稣没有说跟从他就没有轭。他说的是换一个轭：放下原来那个压得人喘不过气的，负起他的。保罗也说，自由是有方向的：“不可将你们的自由当作放纵情欲的机会，总要用爱心互相服事”（加 5:13）。<br>
A yoke is the wooden frame laid on the shoulders of a working animal. Jesus does not say that following him means no yoke. He speaks of exchanging yokes: putting down the one that crushes and taking up his. Paul likewise says that freedom has a direction: “don’t use your freedom for gain to the flesh, but through love be servants to one another” (Galatians 5:13).

不信的人可能会说，这只是换了一个主人。基督徒会同意这个说法，然后补一句：这位主人为他的仆人舍了命。<br>
A sceptic may say this is only a change of masters. A Christian would agree, and then add that this master gave his life for his servants.
