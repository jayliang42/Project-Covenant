# Feasts of the Bible | 圣经中的节期

<a id="in-three-sentences"></a>
> **In Three Sentences | 三句话读懂本篇**
>
> Israel’s calendar was shaped by a weekly day of rest and by yearly feasts that remembered God’s rescue and celebrated the harvest. This page explains the [Sabbath](../Glossary/README.md#sabbath), the feasts of Leviticus 23, and two later festivals: when each falls, what it remembers, and where to read. It also notes how the New Testament connects several feasts with Jesus.<br>
> 以色列的日历由每周的安息日，以及记念神的拯救、庆祝收成的年度节期所塑造。本篇说明[安息日](../Glossary/README.md#sabbath)、利未记 23 章的各个节期，以及后来的两个节日：在什么时候、记念什么、该读哪些经文。也说明新约怎样把其中几个节期与耶稣连在一起。

Israel’s months followed the moon, so these feasts fall on different dates of our calendar each year. The first month of the religious year is in spring. Jewish communities still keep these festivals today; the New Testament connections below are how Christians have read them, not a replacement for their meaning in Israel’s story.<br>
以色列的月份按月亮计算，所以这些节期每年在公历上的日期都不同。宗教年的第一个月在春天。犹太群体今天仍然守这些节期；下面所说的新约关联，是基督徒的理解，并不取代这些节期在以色列故事中的原本意义。

<a id="sabbath"></a>
## The Weekly Sabbath | 每周的安息日

**Read | 阅读：** Exodus 20:8–11; Deuteronomy 5:12–15; Mark 2:23–28 / 出埃及记 20:8–11；申命记 5:12–15；马可福音 2:23–28

Every seventh day Israel stopped work. Exodus grounds this rest in God’s rest after creation; Deuteronomy grounds it in God’s rescue from slavery, so that servants and animals rest too. Jesus taught that the Sabbath was made for people, not people for the Sabbath. Most Christians gather on Sunday to remember the resurrection, while some keep Saturday.<br>
以色列人每到第七日就停工。出埃及记以神创造后的安息作为根据；申命记以神把百姓从奴役中救出作为根据，所以仆人和牲畜也要休息。耶稣教导说，安息日是为人设立的，人不是为安息日设立的。多数基督徒在星期日聚会，记念复活；也有些基督徒守星期六。

<a id="spring"></a>
## Spring Feasts | 春季节期

<a id="passover"></a>
### Passover and Unleavened Bread | 逾越节与除酵节

**Read | 阅读：** Exodus 12:1–28; Leviticus 23:4–8; Luke 22:7–20; 1 Corinthians 5:7–8 / 出埃及记 12:1–28；利未记 23:4–8；路加福音 22:7–20；哥林多前书 5:7–8

On the night of the exodus, each household killed a lamb and marked the doorframe with its blood, and the LORD passed over those homes. For the following week, Israel ate bread made without yeast, remembering how they left Egypt in haste. Jesus’ last meal with his disciples took place at Passover time, and Paul calls Christ “our Passover lamb.”<br>
出埃及的那一夜，各家宰杀羔羊，把血涂在门框上，耶和华就越过那些房屋。接下来的七天，以色列人吃无酵饼，记念他们怎样匆忙离开埃及。耶稣与门徒的最后晚餐正值逾越节期间；保罗称基督是“我们逾越节的羔羊”。

<a id="firstfruits"></a>
### Firstfruits | 初熟节

**Read | 阅读：** Leviticus 23:9–14; 1 Corinthians 15:20–23 / 利未记 23:9–14；哥林多前书 15:20–23

When the barley harvest began, the people brought the first sheaf to the priest before eating any of the new crop, acknowledging that the whole harvest came from God. Paul calls the risen Christ “the firstfruits” of those who will be raised.<br>
大麦开始收割时，百姓在吃新粮之前，先把第一捆禾稼带给祭司，承认整个收成都来自神。保罗称复活的基督为将要复活之人“初熟的果子”。

<a id="weeks"></a>
### Weeks, or Pentecost | 七七节（五旬节）

**Read | 阅读：** Leviticus 23:15–21; Deuteronomy 16:9–12; Acts 2:1–41 / 利未记 23:15–21；申命记 16:9–12；使徒行传 2:1–41

Seven weeks after Firstfruits, at the wheat harvest, Israel brought a new offering and rejoiced together, remembering that they had been slaves in Egypt. The Greek name Pentecost means “fiftieth.” In Acts 2, the Holy Spirit is poured out on Jesus’ followers on this day, while Jews from many nations are in Jerusalem for the feast.<br>
从初熟节算起七个七日之后，正值收割麦子，以色列人献上新的素祭，一同欢乐，并记念自己在埃及作过奴仆。希腊文名称“五旬节”意思是“第五十”。在使徒行传 2 章，圣灵就在这一天浇灌在耶稣的跟随者身上，那时许多国家的犹太人正为守节聚集在耶路撒冷。

<a id="autumn"></a>
## Autumn Feasts | 秋季节期

<a id="trumpets"></a>
### Trumpets | 吹角节

**Read | 阅读：** Leviticus 23:23–25; Numbers 29:1–6 / 利未记 23:23–25；民数记 29:1–6

The seventh month opened with a day of rest and a holy assembly, marked by trumpet blasts. In later Jewish practice this day became Rosh Hashanah, the New Year.<br>
七月初一是一个安息日，要吹角作纪念，并有圣会。在后来的犹太传统中，这一天成为“新年”（Rosh Hashanah）。

<a id="day-of-atonement"></a>
### The Day of Atonement | 赎罪日

**Read | 阅读：** Leviticus 16; Hebrews 9:6–14 / 利未记 16 章；希伯来书 9:6–14

Once a year, the high priest entered the Most Holy Place with blood to make atonement for himself, the people, and the sanctuary. A second goat was sent into the wilderness, carrying the people’s sins away. Hebrews reads this day in light of Jesus, who entered God’s presence “once for all” by his own blood.<br>
每年一次，大祭司带着血进入至圣所，为自己、为百姓、也为圣所赎罪。另一只山羊被送到旷野，象征担当百姓的罪离去。希伯来书从耶稣的角度来读这一天：耶稣凭着自己的血，“只一次”进入神的面前。

<a id="tabernacles"></a>
### Tabernacles, or Booths | 住棚节

**Read | 阅读：** Leviticus 23:33–43; Deuteronomy 16:13–15; Nehemiah 8:13–18; John 7:2, 37–39 / 利未记 23:33–43；申命记 16:13–15；尼希米记 8:13–18；约翰福音 7:2、37–39

After the autumn harvest, Israel lived for seven days in shelters made of branches, remembering how God cared for them in the wilderness, and closed the feast on an eighth day. Deuteronomy commands the people to be joyful at this feast. On its last and greatest day, Jesus stood and called out, inviting anyone who is thirsty to come to him and drink.<br>
秋收之后，以色列人在用树枝搭成的棚里住七天，记念神在旷野中怎样看顾他们，并在第八日结束这个节期。申命记吩咐百姓在这个节期要欢乐。在节期末日最大之日，耶稣站着高声说，人若渴了，可以到祂这里来喝。

<a id="later"></a>
## Later Festivals | 后来的节日

<a id="purim"></a>
### Purim | 普珥节

**Read | 阅读：** Esther 9:20–32 / 以斯帖记 9:20–32

Purim is not in the law of Moses. It celebrates the rescue of the Jews in the Persian Empire in Esther’s day, when a plot to destroy them was reversed. Its name comes from the “lots” Haman cast to choose the day of destruction.<br>
普珥节不在摩西的律法之中。它庆祝以斯帖时代波斯帝国境内的犹太人蒙拯救，一场要灭绝他们的阴谋被扭转。节期的名称来自哈曼为选定灭绝之日所掣的“签”（普珥）。

<a id="dedication"></a>
### Dedication, or Hanukkah | 修殿节

**Read | 阅读：** John 10:22–30; background in 1 Maccabees 4:36–59 / 约翰福音 10:22–30；背景见马加比一书 4:36–59

This winter festival remembers the rededication of the temple after it was defiled under Antiochus IV, in the second century BC. The story is told in 1 Maccabees, a book that Christian traditions place differently in their Bibles. John’s Gospel says Jesus was walking in the temple courts at this feast.<br>
这个冬季节日记念圣殿在主前二世纪被安提阿古四世玷污后重新献给神。故事记在马加比一书中；不同基督教传统对这卷书是否属于圣经有不同认定。约翰福音记载，耶稣曾在这个节期于圣殿里行走。
