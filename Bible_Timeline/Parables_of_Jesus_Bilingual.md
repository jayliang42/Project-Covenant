# Parables of Jesus | 耶稣的比喻

<a id="in-three-sentences"></a>
> **In Three Sentences | 三句话读懂本篇**
>
> Jesus often taught in [parables](../Glossary/README.md#parable): short stories from everyday life that turn a listener’s expectations upside down. This page gives five simple steps for reading a parable, then introduces nineteen well-known parables with where to read and what each one invites us to see. Some parables have more than one careful reading, and the entries say so.<br>
> 耶稣常用[比喻](../Glossary/README.md#parable)教导人：取材于日常生活的短故事，常常颠覆听者原有的想法。本篇先给出读比喻的五个简单步骤，再介绍十九个常见的比喻，写明该读哪段经文、每个比喻邀请我们看见什么。有些比喻有不止一种认真的解读，条目会直接说明。

<a id="how-to-read"></a>
## How to Read a Parable | 怎样读比喻

1. **Notice who is listening.** Jesus often tells a parable in answer to a question or a complaint. Luke 15, for example, begins with religious leaders grumbling that Jesus eats with sinners.
   **留意听众是谁。** 耶稣常常是为了回答一个问题或一句抱怨而讲比喻。例如路加福音 15 章，一开始就是宗教领袖抱怨耶稣和罪人一同吃饭。
2. **Look for the surprise.** A Samaritan is the hero, a father runs to his son, workers who came last are paid first. The surprise usually carries the point.
   **找出意外之处。** 撒玛利亚人成了好人，父亲跑去迎接儿子，最后来的工人最先领工钱——意外之处往往就是重点所在。
3. **Find the main point before the details.** A parable is not a code where every detail stands for something. Ask first what the story as a whole says.
   **先抓重点，再看细节。** 比喻不是密码，不必把每个细节都对应成某样东西。先问整个故事要说什么。
4. **Ask what it shows about God and his kingdom.** Many parables begin “The kingdom of heaven is like…” They show how God reigns and whom he welcomes.
   **问它显明了神和祂的国度什么。** 许多比喻以“天国好像……”开头，说明神怎样掌权、接纳什么样的人。
5. **Ask where you stand in the story.** Parables invite a response. Am I the older brother, the priest who passes by, or the servant who was forgiven much?
   **问自己站在故事的哪里。** 比喻邀请人回应：我是那个大儿子、那个走过去的祭司，还是那个被免了大债的仆人？

<a id="growth"></a>
## Parables of Growth: the Kingdom Like a Seed | 生长的比喻：天国像种子

<a id="sower"></a>
### The Sower | 撒种的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Mark 4:1–20; also Matthew 13 and Luke 8 / 马可福音 4:1–20；另见马太福音 13 章、路加福音 8 章

A farmer scatters seed that falls on a path, rocky ground, thorns, and good soil. The same word of God meets very different hearts, and only some of it takes root and bears a large harvest. Jesus explains this parable himself.<br>
撒种的人撒下种子，落在路旁、石头地、荆棘里和好土里。同样的神的道遇到截然不同的心，只有一部分扎根并结出丰盛的果子。这个比喻耶稣亲自作了解释。

<a id="growing-seed"></a>
### The Growing Seed | 种子长大的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Mark 4:26–29 / 马可福音 4:26–29

A farmer plants seed, sleeps and rises, and the seed grows “he does not know how.” God’s kingdom grows by God’s power, often quietly, before the harvest comes.<br>
人把种子撒在地里，黑夜睡觉，白日起来，种子就发芽渐长，“那人却不晓得如何这样”。神的国借着神的能力生长，常常是静悄悄的，直到收成的时候。

<a id="mustard-seed"></a>
### The Mustard Seed | 芥菜种的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Mark 4:30–32; Matthew 13:31–32 / 马可福音 4:30–32；马太福音 13:31–32

The kingdom begins as small as a tiny seed but grows into a plant large enough for birds to nest in its shade. A small, unimpressive beginning does not decide how great the end will be.<br>
天国起初像一粒极小的种子，却长成大株，飞鸟可以在它的荫下搭窝。起初微小、不起眼，并不决定结局的大小。

<a id="wheat-and-weeds"></a>
### The Wheat and the Weeds | 麦子和稗子的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Matthew 13:24–30 and 36–43 / 马太福音 13:24–30、36–43

An enemy sows weeds among the wheat, but the owner tells his servants to wait until harvest rather than pull them up too early. Good and evil grow side by side for now, and judgment belongs to God at the end.<br>
仇敌在麦子中撒了稗子，主人却吩咐仆人等到收割的时候，不要过早拔除。现今善与恶并存，审判属于神，要在末了进行。

<a id="treasure-and-pearl"></a>
### Hidden Treasure and the Pearl | 藏宝与珠子的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Matthew 13:44–46 / 马太福音 13:44–46

A man finds treasure in a field and, in his joy, sells everything to buy that field. A merchant sells all he has for one pearl of great value. The kingdom is worth more than everything else, and giving up the rest comes from joy, not grim duty.<br>
有人在田里发现宝贝，欢欢喜喜地变卖一切买下那块地；买卖人变卖所有，买下一颗重价的珠子。天国比一切都宝贵；为它放下其余的一切，是出于喜乐，而不是勉强的义务。

## Parables of the Lost and Found | 失而复得的比喻

<a id="lost-sheep-and-coin"></a>
### The Lost Sheep and the Lost Coin | 失羊和失钱的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Luke 15:1–10 / 路加福音 15:1–10

A shepherd leaves ninety-nine sheep to search for one that is lost, and a woman sweeps her whole house for one lost coin. Each calls friends to celebrate. Jesus says heaven rejoices in the same way over one sinner who repents.<br>
牧人撇下九十九只羊去找一只迷失的羊，妇人打扫整间屋子寻找一块失落的钱；找到后都请朋友一同欢喜。耶稣说，一个罪人悔改，天上也要这样欢喜。

![A shepherd carrying a found sheep across his shoulders / 牧人把找回的羊扛在肩上](../assets/art/lost-sheep.svg)

<a id="lost-son"></a>
### The Lost Son and His Brother | 浪子和他哥哥的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Luke 15:11–32 / 路加福音 15:11–32

A younger son wastes his inheritance and comes home hoping only to be a hired worker, but his father runs to embrace him and throws a feast. The older son, who stayed home, refuses to join the celebration. The story ends with the father pleading with him, and we never hear his answer.<br>
小儿子挥霍了家产，回家只盼能作个雇工，父亲却跑去抱住他，为他摆设筵席。一直留在家里的大儿子却不肯进去同乐。故事停在父亲恳劝大儿子的地方，我们始终没有听到他的回答。

<a id="great-banquet"></a>
### The Great Banquet | 大筵席的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Luke 14:15–24 / 路加福音 14:15–24

The invited guests all make excuses, so the host sends his servant into the streets to bring in the poor, the disabled, and the blind. God’s table is open to people the world overlooks, while those who assume they belong may refuse to come.<br>
被请的客人都推辞不来，主人就打发仆人到街上，把贫穷的、残废的、瞎眼的都领进来。神的筵席向世人忽视的人敞开，而那些自以为理所当然有份的人，却可能拒绝赴席。

<a id="mercy-and-prayer"></a>
## Parables of Mercy, Prayer, and Neighbors | 怜悯、祷告与邻舍的比喻

<a id="good-samaritan"></a>
### The Good Samaritan | 好撒玛利亚人的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Luke 10:25–37 / 路加福音 10:25–37

A lawyer asks, “Who is my neighbor?” In Jesus’ story, a priest and a Levite pass by a wounded man, but a Samaritan, someone the listeners would have looked down on, stops and cares for him. Jesus turns the question around: who acted as a neighbor?<br>
一个律法师问：“谁是我的邻舍呢？”在耶稣的故事里，祭司和利未人都从受伤的人身边走过，一个听众所轻看的撒玛利亚人却停下来照顾他。耶稣把问题反过来问：谁是那人的邻舍？

<a id="unmerciful-servant"></a>
### The Unmerciful Servant | 不饶恕人的仆人的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Matthew 18:21–35 / 马太福音 18:21–35

A servant is forgiven an enormous debt by his king, then has a fellow servant thrown into prison over a small one. Those who have received God’s great mercy are called to forgive others from the heart.<br>
一个仆人欠王一笔巨债蒙了免除，转身却因一笔小债把同伴下在监里。领受了神极大怜悯的人，蒙召要从心里饶恕别人。

<a id="pharisee-and-tax-collector"></a>
### The Pharisee and the Tax Collector | 法利赛人和税吏的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Luke 18:9–14 / 路加福音 18:9–14

A respected religious man thanks God that he is not like other people, while a despised tax collector stands far off and prays, “God, have mercy on me, a sinner.” Jesus says it is the tax collector who goes home justified.<br>
一个受人尊敬的宗教人士感谢神自己不像别人，一个被人看不起的税吏却远远站着祷告说：“神啊，开恩可怜我这个罪人！”耶稣说，回家时被算为义的是这个税吏。

<a id="persistent-widow"></a>
### The Persistent Widow | 恒切祷告的寡妇的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Luke 18:1–8 / 路加福音 18:1–8

A widow keeps coming to an unjust judge until he finally gives her justice just to be rid of her. If even such a judge responds, how much more will God, who is not unjust, hear his people who cry out to him? Luke says the point is to pray always and not lose heart.<br>
一个寡妇不断去找一个不义的官，那官终于为她伸冤，只为免得她常来搅扰。连这样的官都会回应，何况那位并非不义的神，岂不更听祂子民的呼求？路加说，这比喻的重点是要常常祷告，不可灰心。

<a id="rich-fool"></a>
### The Rich Fool | 无知财主的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Luke 12:13–21 / 路加福音 12:13–21

A rich man plans bigger barns so he can take life easy for many years, but that very night his life is required of him. Life does not consist in possessions; the question is whether we are rich toward God.<br>
一个财主打算盖更大的仓房，好安安逸逸地享受多年，当夜他的灵魂却被取去。人的生命不在乎家道丰富；问题在于我们是否在神面前富足。

<a id="workers-in-vineyard"></a>
### The Workers in the Vineyard | 葡萄园工人的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Matthew 20:1–16 / 马太福音 20:1–16

Workers hired at the end of the day receive the same wage as those who worked since dawn, and the early workers complain. The owner answers that he has been fair to them and generous to the others. God’s grace is not a payment earned by hours worked.<br>
傍晚才雇来的工人和清早就做工的人领一样的工钱，早来的人就埋怨。家主回答说，他对他们并没有亏负，对别人则是慷慨。神的恩典不是按工时计算的报酬。

<a id="readiness"></a>
## Parables of Readiness and the Coming King | 警醒与君王再来的比喻

<a id="wise-and-foolish-builders"></a>
### The Wise and Foolish Builders | 盖房子在磐石上的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Matthew 7:24–27 / 马太福音 7:24–27

At the end of the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus compares two houses: one built on rock, one on sand. When the storm comes, only the house built on hearing and doing his words stands.<br>
在登山宝训的结尾，耶稣比较两座房子：一座盖在磐石上，一座盖在沙土上。风暴来时，只有那座建在“听了又去行”之上的房子站立得住。

<a id="wicked-tenants"></a>
### The Wicked Tenants | 凶恶园户的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Mark 12:1–12 / 马可福音 12:1–12

Tenants of a vineyard beat the owner’s servants and finally kill his beloved son. The religious leaders recognize that Jesus has told this story against them. It looks back on Israel’s prophets and ahead to Jesus’ own death.<br>
葡萄园的园户打了园主差来的仆人，最后杀了他的爱子。宗教领袖看出这个比喻是指着他们说的。它回顾以色列先知的遭遇，也预告耶稣自己的死。

<a id="ten-virgins"></a>
### The Ten Bridesmaids | 十个童女的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Matthew 25:1–13 / 马太福音 25:1–13

Ten young women wait for a bridegroom who is delayed. Five brought extra oil for their lamps and five did not, and only the prepared go in to the wedding feast. Jesus’ followers are to stay ready for his return, even when it seems slow in coming.<br>
十个童女等候迟延的新郎。五个预备了灯油，五个没有，只有预备好的进去赴婚宴。耶稣的跟随者要时刻预备迎接祂再来，即使看起来迟延。

<a id="talents"></a>
### The Talents | 按才干受托的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Matthew 25:14–30; compare Luke 19:11–27 / 马太福音 25:14–30；可对照路加福音 19:11–27

A master entrusts large sums to three servants before a journey. Two put the money to work; the third buries it out of fear. Many read this as a call to use faithfully everything God entrusts to us; others focus on the third servant’s distorted view of his master. Both readings take the master’s return seriously.<br>
主人远行前把大笔银子交给三个仆人。两个拿去经营，第三个因为害怕就把它埋起来。许多人把它读作呼召：忠心使用神交托的一切；也有人着重于第三个仆人对主人的扭曲认识。两种读法都认真看待主人的归来。

<a id="sheep-and-goats"></a>
### The Sheep and the Goats | 绵羊和山羊的比喻

**Read | 阅读：** Matthew 25:31–46 / 马太福音 25:31–46

When the Son of Man comes as king, he separates people as a shepherd separates sheep from goats. He says that whatever was done for “the least of these brothers and sisters of mine” was done for him. Readers differ on whether “the least of these” means anyone in need or especially Jesus’ followers. Some call this passage a judgment scene rather than a parable.<br>
人子作王降临时，要把人分开，好像牧人分别绵羊和山羊。祂说，人在“我这弟兄中一个最小的”身上所做的，就是做在祂身上。读者对“最小的”是指一切有需要的人、还是特别指耶稣的门徒，看法不同；也有人认为这段是审判的场景，而不算比喻。
